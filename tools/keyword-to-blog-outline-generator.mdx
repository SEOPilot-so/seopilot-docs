The Keyword to Blog Outline Generator takes a target keyword and returns a full article outline — including suggested H2 and H3 headings — so you can start writing with a clear structure instead of a blank page.

## How to use it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the tool">
    Go to [seopilot.so/tools/keyword-to-blog-outline-generator](https://seopilot.so/tools/keyword-to-blog-outline-generator).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your keyword or topic">
    Type in the keyword or topic you're targeting — for example, "programmatic seo for saas".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate your outline">
    Click **Generate outline** to receive a full article structure built around your keyword.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the outline as your skeleton">
    Take the generated outline into your editor or SEOPilot workflow and start filling in each section.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the outline includes

The generator produces a complete article framework you can use straight away. Each outline includes:

- A **suggested article title** optimized for the keyword.
- An **intro premise** — a framing sentence or two that sets up what the article covers and why it matters.
- **4–6 H2 sections** covering the main topics a reader would expect the article to address.
- **H3 sub-sections** under each H2 to break down more specific points within each topic.
- A **FAQ section** drawn from common questions related to the keyword.

You can use the outline as-is or rearrange sections to match your specific angle — the structure is a starting point, not a constraint.

## Using the outline with SEOPilot

If you're a SEOPilot subscriber, you're not limited to the outline the tool generates. From your dashboard, you can submit a custom topic with your own preferred outline structure — your chosen H2s, sub-sections, and any specific points you want covered. SEOPilot will use your headings as a guide when drafting the full article, so the output matches your editorial thinking rather than a generic structure.

<Tip>
  The outline is most useful as a starting checklist — go through each section and verify it matches the search intent for your keyword. If the SERP for your target query is dominated by listicles, add list-style H2s. If it's full of step-by-step how-tos, structure your outline around sequential steps. Matching the dominant format on the SERP is one of the clearest signals you can send that your article belongs there.
</Tip>
